## 1. Summary

Combined Synopsis/Solicitation for mattresses for NOAA Ship Rainier, a
hydrographic survey vessel homeported in Newport, OR (~45 berths).
Posted 2026-08-07, due 2026-08-24, NAICS 449110 + PSC 7110. Surfaced by
the 2026-08-10 SAM ingest at fit 80; triage kept it watching pending a
signed-in SAM read of the attachments (public page JS-gated, no mirror
coverage yet).

## 2. Disqualifying factors

- **Primary reason:** Operator no-bid call, 2026-08-10, in the digest
  triage review - directed that all new opportunities from the 08-10
  cycle be rejected rather than spending the signed-in spec read.
- **Contributing reasons:** Small shipboard buy (one vessel, ~45
  berths) with delivery in Newport, OR - the same
  micro-quantity-plus-long-freight economics that no-bid the 19-unit
  Barbers Point HI order the same day. Unresolved spec risk on top:
  ship berths are typically custom dimensions and the buy may require
  a maritime fire cert (IMO/SOLAS) CSP does not hold, rather than
  16 CFR 1633.

## 3. What would change a future bid/no-bid call

- A NOAA/OMAO or other vessel-support solicitation with standard
  mattress sizes, a 16 CFR 1633 (not maritime) fire spec, and a
  quantity or delivery point that clears the freight math.
- A fleet-wide or multi-ship berthing recompete rather than a
  single-vessel replacement buy.

## 4. Lessons captured

- Shipboard "mattresses" titles stay a two-sided trap: the product
  term is right, but custom berth dimensions and maritime fire certs
  can each independently kill fit - and confirming either requires the
  login-gated attachments, so the spec read has a real cost that a
  small single-vessel quantity may not justify.
