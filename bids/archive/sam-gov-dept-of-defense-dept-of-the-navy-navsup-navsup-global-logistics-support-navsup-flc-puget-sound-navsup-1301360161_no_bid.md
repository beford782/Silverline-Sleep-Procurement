## 1. Summary

Sources Sought for brand-name OEM (multiple) procurement of "Standard
Beds, Box Springs, Mattresses & Headboards" aboard a naval vessel —
in full-spec review, USS Mustin habitability outfitting via SRF-JRMC,
place of performance Japan. 32 line items of shipboard aluminum
berths, bed assemblies, wardrobes, and lavatory units built to Navy
805-/804-series drawings with MIL-PRF-24712 powder coat and
MIL-C-24500 curtains, restricted to 10 named marine-habitability OEMs
(or-equal).

## 2. Disqualifying factors

- **Primary reason:** Fabricated marine metal furniture to Navy
  drawing specs is not a Continental Silverline product, and CSP
  holds none of the 10 named OEM authorizations.
- **Contributing reasons:** Only line item 1 includes a mattress at
  all, as part of a drawing-spec bed assembly — no separable
  mattress/bedding line to quote; delivery is OCONUS (Japan); the
  notice states no feedback is given to Sources Sought responses, so
  a response had no positioning value.

## 3. What would change a future bid/no-bid call

- A Navy solicitation with a separable shipboard mattress line built
  to a commercial or MIL mattress spec (rather than an integrated
  drawing-spec berth assembly), delivered CONUS.
- Marine-habitability OEM partnership/authorization, which is not
  currently pursued.

## 4. Lessons captured

- "Mattresses" in shipboard-outfitting titles usually means integrated
  berth assemblies under fabricated-metal NAICS (337127) — check for a
  separable mattress line before deeper triage.
- The mattress-relevant title masked a 32-line habitability package;
  reading the notice body caught it. Attachments were never posted —
  the spec lived in the body text.
