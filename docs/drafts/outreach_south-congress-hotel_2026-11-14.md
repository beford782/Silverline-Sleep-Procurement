## Buyer brief

- **Project status:** Live, but it is not what the row says. This is no longer an independent hotel. Hyatt announced on 2026-04-01 that the property is being converted into **The Standard, Austin**, a Hyatt lifestyle brand, opening Spring 2027 [1]. The closure for the work was planned for around 2026-05-31, with most or all of the staff laid off: one outlet says all 126 people [3], another says "most", with key roles kept [4]. No article written after May confirms the closing date, but the October permits and the brand's "Coming 2027" page [10] make it very likely the hotel is closed. Six City of Austin building permits were issued 2026-10-27, all listing Structure Tone as general contractor [6]. Room count: 83 for the old hotel [5], 82 on The Standard's own page [10].
  - **Are guest rooms in scope? Probably yes for the project, no for these permits.** One local article, written the day before Hyatt's announcement, says in the reporter's own words that visitors can expect "a full redesign of all guestrooms" [2]. Hyatt's release is vaguer: it says only that guest rooms will have "a global sensibility brought to life with local craftsmanship" [1]. So a full room redesign rests on one source. The six permits cover something else: first-floor common areas (17,000 sq ft), two retail shells (1,798 and 905 sq ft), a restaurant/bar (2,830 sq ft), and "hotel areas" on floor 2 (2,500 sq ft) and floor 3 (1,146 sq ft) [6]. That is about 26,200 sq ft in total, and the upper-floor pieces are far too small to be 83 rooms. The room refresh is probably furniture-and-finishes work that needs no building permit, but that is my reading, not a stated fact.

- **Route: brand program.** Hyatt, alone or with Timberline, has acquired the building and is putting its own brand on it, with the rooms designed by The Standard's in-house design team and a New York design studio, so beds will be chosen by the brand and its designers, not by anyone at the property.
  - **Do not email the hotel.** There is very likely no hotel staff to email: the layoffs were planned for around May 31, and no new general manager has been named. The brand does publish a general inbox for the hotel, austin@standardhotels.com [10], but that is a guest-facing brand inbox, not a bed buyer.
  - **Better route:** the Hyatt Lifestyle Group / The Standard design team (who set what goes in the room), and Hyatt's purchasing channel. Third-party guides say Hyatt buys operating supplies, including bedding, through a purchasing company called Avendra; I could not confirm that on an official Hyatt or Avendra page, so treat it as a lead to check, not a fact [8].
  - **Honest caveat:** I found no evidence that The Standard has a fixed national mattress supplier. "Brand program" here means "the brand decides", which is confirmed; "nationally sourced" is likely but not proven.
